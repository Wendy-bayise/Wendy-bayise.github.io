Agile team charter

The team’s agreement on purpose, roles and ways of working. Group 3D, PRP372S: Emihle Mbada (Product Owner), Wendy Bayise (Agile Lead), Paul and Wokholo. Click any page to read it full size.

Reflection (STAR)

Situation Our group of four had to deliver a sentiment analysis web app in two one-week sprints, with a heavy academic workload, no budget and uneven skill levels.

Task Agree how we would work together before building anything, so that everyone knew their role, how we would communicate and what “done” meant.

Action We wrote the charter together: a mission and success definition, four roles (Product Owner, Agile Lead, UI/UX designer and front-end developer, developer and tester), daily stand-ups and weekly planning, response-time rules, a Definition of Done, and a list of risks and constraints.

Result Nobody had to guess who owned what, and the risks we named early (missed deadlines, miscommunication, limited tools) were easier to manage. Next time we would tick our decision-making method, schedule the first charter review earlier and have all four members sign the commitment page.

Agile logbook

The weekly logbook: the taskboard (backlog, in progress, completed) and a walkthrough covering progress, blockers and the team goal.

Reflection (STAR)

Situation With four members working remotely around other modules, we needed a way to see progress and problems every day without long meetings.

Task Keep a weekly logbook that tracked tasks, showed who was doing what, and surfaced blockers early.

Action We used a taskboard (backlog, in progress, completed) and answered three stand-up questions each day: what I finished, what I plan next and what is blocking me. We also completed a team walkthrough covering our goal, bottlenecks and whether anyone was working off the board.

Result Progress stayed visible, everyone confirmed they were working only on board tasks, and blockers such as limited time, internet problems and data quality were written down before they grew. Next time we would date every entry and link each line to its Trello card.

Reflection (STAR)

Situation We had nine user stories and ten days of planned work, and needed one place where the whole team could see what was planned, in progress and finished.

Task Build a Trello board that connected the backlog to the daily sprint work, with clear ownership and a clear finish line for every card.

Action We created three lists (Product Backlog, Sprint 1, Sprint 2) and gave each daily card a goal, a deliverable, the user stories it supports, labels, a due date, both assigned members and a checklist. A card was only completed once its checklist was ticked.

Result Every card ended complete with a 4/4 checklist, and the work could be traced from backlog to delivery. Next time we would add short comments to cards when plans change so the reasoning behind decisions is not lost.

Reflection (STAR)

Situation The project had to be finished in two one-week sprints, so there was little room for wasted effort or unclear priorities.

Task Plan each day so that the team had a single focus and a visible result.

Action Sprint 1 covered the problem definition, charter, research, feasibility and risk analysis, and wireframes with database design. Sprint 2 built and tested the features day by day and ended with fixes and the final demo.

Result Because research and design were done first, Sprint 2 could concentrate on building, and each stand-up was specific. Next time we would leave a buffer day, since the last day carried testing, fixes and the demo together.

Reflection (STAR)

Situation The app had to meet real user needs, not just work technically, and we had to prove it in a short timeframe.

Task Turn the requirements into clear user stories with acceptance criteria, then build and verify every one of them.

Action We designed all nine stories in Sprint 1, Day 5, grouped related ones into the same build day (for example delete, clear and export), tested each against its acceptance criteria, and gave all nine a final check on Sprint 2, Day 5.

Result All nine stories were completed. Handling unclear and mixed comments (US-3) showed us that real input rarely fits neatly into positive or negative. Next time we would add edge-case stories, such as empty or very long comments, and test with real users earlier.

Task Present myself professionally on camera and answer the questions well.

Action I chose the questions most suitable for me and prepared my answers using the STAR method. I wrote key points for each question instead of a full script, so that I would sound natural and not read off a paper.
